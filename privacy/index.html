TapCount — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-31

In short: TapCount does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. Your records are stored locally on your device; copies can leave it when you export them or through backups managed by your operating system.

Data storage

Your counters and tap history are stored locally on your device in a JSON file. On iOS this file lives in an App Group container shared only with the TapCount home-screen widget; on Android it lives in app-private storage readable by TapCount and its own home-screen widget. Your counter data can leave the device when you export it or through device backups managed by Apple or Google, as described below. TapCount does not send it to us.

Export & import

The export feature creates a JSON file that you share via the system share sheet to a destination you choose. TapCount has no server and never receives a copy.

Notifications

Inactivity reminders are scheduled locally on your device. No notification data is sent to us or to third parties.

Analytics & third parties

TapCount contains no analytics, no advertising SDKs, and no third-party tracking of any kind.

In-app purchase

TapCount Premium is a one-time purchase handled entirely by the Apple App Store or Google Play. TapCount never sees your payment details; it only receives a confirmation that the purchase exists, which unlocks the widget and reminders on your device.

Device backups

Your data is included in your device's own backups (iCloud / iTunes backup on iOS, Android's automatic backup to your Google account), so it can come back when you restore or change phone. These backups are managed by Apple or Google under your account; TapCount has no access to them.

Network access

TapCount makes no network requests of its own, and fonts are bundled inside the app. Buying or restoring Premium talks to the App Store or Google Play. The "Rate TapCount" and "Privacy policy" buttons in Settings open the App Store or Google Play app (or your browser) outside TapCount; those are not TapCount itself making a network request. After enough use, the app may also ask your operating system to show its native store review sheet once; the system handles that prompt, and TapCount sends no data to do so.
